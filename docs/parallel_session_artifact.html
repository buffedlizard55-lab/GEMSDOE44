What this is

A fault-discovery system for the DOE GEMS Prize (DrivenData #306). The task is to predict, as a single-band GeoTIFF of probabilities in [0, 1], where geological faults are in the GeoDAWN region of Nevada — and specifically where the faults are that the public USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue does not contain, because that is what the competition scores.

The scoring rules that decide everything

Read from the official problem description on 2026-10-06 (page 967): the metric is a distance-weighted Tversky index with a triangular kernel of 300 m support, α = 0.2 on false positives and β = 0.8 on false negatives. DrivenData staff confirmed in thread 11516 that pixels on known USGS/INGENIOUS faults are excluded from evaluation — so a dot placed on a known fault is free but worthless.

Three consequences, each verified in tests/test_metric.py rather than asserted:

  1. FN_w = |G| − TP_w exactly, so the metric reduces to DTI = TP_w / (0.2·(TP_w + FP_w) + 0.8·|G|). Writing T = TP_w and S for the emitted mass, DTI ≈ T / (0.2·S + 0.8·|G|).
  2. A binary (0, 1) file strictly dominates any graded version of itself (the score is increasing in the scale of a probability map). Every artifact here is binary.
  3. The marginal rule: adding a dot of kernel credit k pays iff k > 0.2 · DTI. At a score of 0.3345 that is a distance of at most 280 m from a scored truth pixel. Credit is nearly free anywhere inside the kernel and worthless outside it — the metric is a distance filter, not a precision filter.

Why the previous best artifact scored what it scored — measured, not asserted

Two artifacts exist locally whose live scores bracket the family's best work: the 44,090-dot artifact the live board reports at 0.2600, and the 37,654-dot artifact its owner reports at 0.2778. They are related byte for byte: the 0.2778 file is a strict subset of the 0.2600 file, with 6,436 dots deleted and none added, and 100% of the deleted dots lie within 200 m of the known catalogue — the 0–100 m and 100–200 m bands are emptied to exactly zero. On the local truth frame that passes the admissibility test below, those 6,436 dots earned a mean kernel credit of 0.0135 against their 0.2-per-dot cost: a net −1,187 units of denominator mass. That is the mechanism of the 0.2600 → 0.2778 step, reproduced first-hand rather than quoted, and it is why this repository's emitter now excludes that ring (--ring-px 2).

The same comparison falsifies the frame this repository had pre-registered as primary. Frame N (SGMC strands within 300 m of the catalogue) scores the pair backwards: 0.1755 for the 0.2600 artifact versus 0.0541 for the better 0.2778 one. Frame P (SGMC strands beyond 300 m) ranks them correctly (0.0954 > 0.0942). A frame that ranks the family's best-known pair the wrong way cannot select a submission, so the admissibility rule fixed in AM-44-03 — rank the pair correctly and have positive probe excess over the 19 live-scored artifacts — excludes N and admits P. Both facts are on the limitations page as IR-44-15.

  • It re-picks the field on the admissible frame, and the answer flips. On frame N the winner is catalogue proximity (prox10, mean fold DTI 0.3964 against the incumbent's 0.1356). On frame P the order inverts: the field trained on all SGMC strands the catalogue lacks (sup_U) wins with a mean fold excess of +0.0808 over the uniform control and the best-of-4-folds positive in 4 of 4 folds at every mass tested, while catalogue proximity (prox10) is worse than uniform (−0.0342). The shipped field is therefore the unblended supervised one, emitted with the ring excluded. Evidence: registry/selection.json, registry/ring_experiment.json.
  • It excludes the ring only because the admissible frame says so. At matched mass and the same field, excluding the ≤200 m ring changes frame P by +0.0109 (sup_U, 44,090 dots) and by +0.02 to +0.05 for the other fields tested; the paired delta is positive for every field and mass tested. On the falsified frame N the same change is −0.075, which is precisely why the frame question had to be settled first. Evidence: registry/ring_experiment.json.
  • It emits at the metric's own spacing. Across the same 19 artifacts, one of the strongest measured correlates of a reported score is dot spacing: Spearman(score, fraction of dots 8-adjacent to another dot) = −0.361 — the three best artifacts have 0.001–0.002 adjacent dots against 0.88–0.99 for the worst; this artifact has 0.00098. The ring profile is the second: the family's best artifact and both sibling-session artifacts all sit at 0.000 / 0.002 / 0.057 / 0.941 in the 0–100 / 100–200 / 200–300 / beyond-300 m bands. Mass also correlates (ρ = −0.653) but is confounded with the family's design eras, which is why the shipped mass is matched to the incumbent's 44,090 for a clean comparison. The mechanism is exact under the official formula: a dot beside another adds no new truth coverage and still costs 0.2 of FP. The emitter here is an exact greedy max-coverage solver that refuses any dot adding no new kernel credit.
  • It emits with the metric's own arithmetic. Because TP_w is a max over a 300 m disc, expected credit is submodular in the emitted set, so a fixed-order greedy emitter carries the standard (1 − 1/e) guarantee, and the stopping condition is the closed-form break-even rule above rather than a tuned radius.

Honest limitations
